📖 Metrics Glossary — what every column / number means (click to expand)
📊 Headline metrics
  • Composite — KEEP/DISCARD decision metric. Formula: min(test_sharpe, val_sharpe) − 0.05 × n_negative_folds. Punishes models that look great on test but fail on val (or vice-versa) AND models with regime-specific failures. Higher = better.
  • Test Sharpe — annualised Sharpe ratio on the held-out test fold (union of 7 regime windows: GFC, EU debt, taper, China-oil, vol-mageddon, COVID, AI rally). √252 × mean(daily_returns) / std(daily_returns). Sharpe > 1 = strong; > 2 = exceptional; QQQ buy-and-hold ≈ 1.22.
  • Val Sharpe — Sharpe on validation set (used for early-stopping + composite). Strong divergence from test Sharpe = overfitting.
  • Train Sharpe — Sharpe on training data. Diagnostic only — train sharpe much greater than test sharpe = severe overfit.
  • $Equity — final portfolio value if you started with $1,000 and traded the strategy across all 7 test folds. $3,144 in exp 52 = $1k → $3.1k = +214%.
🎯 Direction-prediction (classification)
  • Hit% — directional accuracy: percent of trade-days where sign(prediction) = sign(actual_return). 50% = coin-flip; 52-55% in finance is genuinely tradeable.
  • Prec / Rec — Precision / Recall on the UP-direction class. Precision = "of all UP predictions, how many were right." Recall = "of all actual UP days, how many did we catch."
  • F1 / F2 — F-scores. F1 = harmonic mean of precision and recall. F2 weights recall 2× more than precision (penalises missed UP-days harder; useful in trending markets).
  • MCC — Matthews Correlation Coefficient, a balanced accuracy metric robust to class imbalance. Range −1 to +1; 0 = random; > 0.1 = real signal in finance.
  • Acc — overall accuracy = (TP + TN) / total. Same as hit% but explicit.
📐 Statistical reliability
  • PSR — Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2012). Probability that the OBSERVED Sharpe is greater than 0 given the sample size, skewness, and kurtosis. PSR > 0.95 = "very confident the Sharpe is positive, not noise." PSR = 1.000 means we are statistically certain. Critical because in-sample Sharpes can be inflated by skew/kurtosis or short windows.
  • IC — Information Coefficient = Pearson correlation between predicted return and actual return. Range −1 to +1. > 0.05 = real signal in equity prediction; > 0.10 = strong (Grinold-Kahn rule of thumb).
  • excess_sharpe — Strategy Sharpe MINUS buy-and-hold Sharpe over the same window. >0 = strategy beats holding the index. exp 52 +0.10 means +0.10 Sharpe over QQQ buy-and-hold (1.22 → 1.32).
🤖 Uncertainty heads (Kendall-Gal 2017)
  • Conf — Confidence = 1 − epistemic_uncertainty. The model's "I know the answer" signal. High conf + correct = good; high conf + wrong = dangerous overconfidence.
  • Ale — Aleatoric uncertainty (data noise estimate). Predicted log-variance from the het-loss head, exp-transformed. High aleatoric on a regime = "this period is genuinely noisy / unpredictable" (e.g. crisis windows). Optimal range 0.05-0.15.
  • epistemic — Model's "what I don't know yet" signal (typically MC-Dropout variance). High epistemic on a regime = "I haven't seen enough data of this type" — useful for regime-shift detection.
🧪 Experiment tracking
  • T+/V+ — count of positive folds in TEST / VALIDATION. 7/7 on test = perfect regime coverage; 3/7 = model fails on 4 of 7 regimes. Critical for ensemble and risk control.
  • Status — KEEP (composite improved) / DISCARD (composite regressed) / CRASH (training failed).
  • Time — wall-clock training time. ~30 min typical for Mamba 100-epoch run on RTX 4090 Laptop.
🎯 Targets (A / B / D)
  • A — fwd_ret_1d (PRIMARY) — 1-day forward log-return. Drives KEEP/DISCARD. The trade is realised on this return.
  • B — fwd_ret_5d — 5-day forward return (auxiliary head, jointly trained). Tracked because longer horizons are sometimes more stable.
  • D — vol-adjusted 1d — fwd_ret_1d / rolling_vol_20. Orthogonalises trend from skill — high D-Sharpe = real edge, not just trend-following. The TRADE is always on raw 1d return; D's prediction only sets DIRECTION.

🎛️ Decision rules (CLAUDE.md research-strict)
  • KEEP — composite > previous global champion AND all 7 test folds positive.
  • NEAR-MISS — composite within 0.12 of champion (between 1.20 and 1.32 currently). Worth multi-seed lock.
  • DISCARD — otherwise. Composite + per-fold both regressed.
  • Multi-seed lock — single-seed lifts require ≥3 seeds with median > baseline before declaring champion. dmamba seed=42 has a "lucky basin" — both seq=90 (+0.96) and bs=16 (+1.51) at single-seed COLLAPSED to 3-seed median +0.14 / -0.24. Multi-seed median is the credible metric.
🛠 Trading Strategies — Smart Overlays (Tier S)
  • raw — sign(signal), 1-unit position, daily rebalance. The vanilla baseline. Pros: simple. Cons: high turnover, pays bid-ask + spread on every flip; vulnerable to whipsaw. Reference: Sharpe 1966 J. Business 'Mutual fund performance'.
  • hold_until_flip — Hold the position until the signal direction changes. Cuts turnover by ~50% if the signal is autocorrelated day-to-day, saves ~2-4% per year on transaction costs. When it wins: momentum signals that persist multi-day. When it loses: mean-reverting signals that flip more usefully than the rule allows. Reference: Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012 JFE 'Time series momentum' (arXiv:1201.5333).
  • dead_zone — Skip days when |signal| < median(|signal|). Trades only when the model is more confident than its typical day. Lifts hit-rate from ~52% to ~58-62% on traded days. Tradeoff: ~30% fewer trades; fewer compounding opportunities. Reference: Lim, Zohren & Roberts 2019 J. Financial Data Sci. 'Enhancing Time Series Momentum' (arXiv:1906.04025) §4.2.
  • vol_regime_gate_15 — Trade only when QQQ's realized 60-day vol > 15% (annualized). The intuition: alpha concentrates in volatile regimes (GFC, EU debt, COVID, vol-mageddon). In calm bull regimes (2017-2019, 2024-2025), the model is signal-poor and bleeds to costs. Empirical proof: QQQ's existing +0.368 ensemble champion uses exactly this gate (rvol60d > 15%); the gate adds +0.234 Sharpe. Reference: Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou 2009 RFS 'Expected Stock Returns and Variance Risk Premia'.
  • sma200_real — Only LONG when QQQ closes above its 200-day SMA (real prices, not a proxy). SHORT signals always pass through. Skips bullish trades during corrective regimes; the 200d SMA is a coarse uptrend gauge. Reference: Faber 2007 J. Wealth Management 'A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation' (DOI 10.3905/jwm.2007.690942).
  • quarter_kelly — Position size = 0.25 × Sharpe² (capped at 1.0 of capital). Theoretically optimal log-utility growth. Quarter-Kelly (vs full Kelly) reduces ruin risk under estimation error. When it wins: high-Sharpe strategies (full Kelly proportional to Sharpe²) get amplified. When it loses: negative-Sharpe → 0 position. Reference: Kelly 1956 Bell Sys Tech J 'A New Interpretation of Information Rate'; Thorp 1969 'Optimal Gambling Systems for Favorable Games'.
  • vol_target_15pct — Scale position by min(2x, 15% / realized_vol_20). Maintains constant ex-ante portfolio volatility regardless of regime. Juices returns in calm regimes (more leverage), dampens in volatile (less). Capped at 2x leverage to avoid blow-up. Reference: Lim, Zohren & Roberts 2019 (arXiv:1906.04025) §3.1; Moreira & Muir 2017 J. Finance 'Volatility-Managed Portfolios'.
  • confidence_weighted — Continuous position size = signal/max(|signal|), bounded in [-1, +1]. High-conviction days get full size; weak signals get scaled-down position. Implicit dead-zone via the magnitude scaling. Reference: López de Prado 2018 'Advances in Financial ML' §10 conviction-weighted sizing.
🛡 Trading Strategies — Hedging (Tier H, Options + Stock)
  • covered_call — On bullish signal: long QQQ + sell a 30-day, 5%-OTM call. Collects the call premium as income; caps upside at +5%. When it wins: flat-to-mildly-bullish regimes — premium income is pure profit. When it loses: strong rally above the strike (you forfeit gains above 5%). Daily P&L = QQQ return + (BS_call_price / 30 / spot). Reference: Whaley 2002 J. Derivatives 'Risk and Return of the CBOE BuyWrite Monthly Index'; CBOE BXM index methodology.
  • protective_put — On bullish signal: long QQQ + buy a 30-day, 5%-OTM put as crash insurance. Caps downside at ~-5% per period; pays insurance premium. When it wins: tail-risk events when the put pays off (e.g., COVID crash, Vol-mageddon). When it loses: calm regimes — the put expires worthless and you've burned the premium. Daily P&L = max(SPY_return, -5%/30) - (BS_put_price / 30 / spot). Reference: Bookstaber & Clarke 1985 'Problems in Evaluating the Performance of Portfolios with Options'; Israelov & Klein 2016 'Risk and Return of Equity Index Collar Strategies'.
  • collar — Long QQQ + protective put + covered call (stack of the two above). Returns are bounded in [-5%, +5%] per period. Net premium can be near-zero (call premium funds the put). When it wins: high-vol environments where the put pays off and the call premium is rich. When it loses: trending markets — capped on both sides. Reference: Israelov & Klein 2016 J. Investment Mgmt; Whaley 2002.
  • cash_secured_put — On bullish signal: sell a 30-day, 5%-OTM cash-secured put. Collect the premium; if QQQ drops > 5%, you're assigned (buy the dip). When it wins: sideways to mildly bearish — premium is profit. When it loses: sharp drawdowns > 5% — assignment buys at high relative cost. Daily P&L = (BS_put_price / 30 / spot) + min(0, SPY_return + 5%). Reference: McMillan 2002 'Options as a Strategic Investment' §15; CBOE PUT index.
  • vertical_call_spread — On bullish signal: long ATM call + short 5%-OTM call. Defined risk (cost = net debit), defined reward (max payout = 5% strike width). ~3x leverage on the move within the strikes. When it wins: moderate bullish moves of 2-5%. When it loses: small moves (theta decay) or huge moves above upper strike (capped). Reference: Hull 2018 'Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives' §11.3.
  • synthetic_long — Long ATM call + short ATM put = synthetic long stock at zero net premium (put-call parity). Captures full QQQ upside and downside, plus dividend carry (~1.8% annual). When it wins: when long stock would, but with leverage from low capital outlay. When it loses: when long stock would. Reference: Stoll 1969 J. Finance 'The Relationship Between Put and Call Option Prices'; put-call parity.
📐 Position Sizing — How Big to Bet
  • Full Kelly — `f* = Î¼ / Ïƒ²` for log-utility. Optimal compounded growth IF you know Î¼ and Ïƒ exactly. Real life: always overestimates. Use fractional Kelly.
  • Quarter Kelly — 25% of full Kelly. Standard professional practice (Thorp). Trades 50% of optimal growth for 75% reduction in drawdown variance.
  • Volatility targeting — Position = target_vol / realized_vol. Maintains constant ex-ante portfolio volatility. Increases leverage in calm regimes, deleverages in volatile ones. Capped at 2x to avoid blow-up.
  • Conviction-weighted — Position proportional to |signal|. Large bets when model is confident, small when uncertain.
  • Equal-weight — Same position regardless of confidence. Robust to forecast error but leaves alpha on the table.
  • Risk parity — Allocate inverse-variance across multiple strategies in an ensemble. Each contributes equal risk to the portfolio. Reference: Roncalli 2014 'Introduction to Risk Parity and Budgeting'.
🎯 Ensemble Aggregation — Combining Models
  • mean — Unweighted average of member predictions. Simple, robust, but gives equal voice to weak members. Best when members have similar quality.
  • vote — Sum of member directions, sign(sum) = direction. Robust to outlier predictions. Loses magnitude information. Top-K vote (only top members vote) is a common production strategy. Reference: Bates & Granger 1969 'The Combination of Forecasts'.
  • weighted (by-X) — Weight each member by metric X (e.g. by-Sharpe, by-return, by-PSR). Better-than-equal but selection bias risk if X is computed on the same window. Use cross-validation when possible.
  • vote_geq_K — Only trade when ≥K of N members agree. High-confidence filter. Lifts hit-rate, lowers exposure. Tradeoff: fewer trades.
  • family_only — Use only members of one architecture family (e.g., all-MLP). Diagnostic — does the family carry signal alone?
  • best1_by_X — Use a single member, the best by metric X. Baseline for comparing whether ensembling actually helps.
🔢 Selection Criteria — How to Pick Members
  • by_oos_sharpe — Highest individual OOS annualised Sharpe. Standard quant-finance ranking.
  • by_oos_return — Highest total $ return. Direct dollar-maximization. May favor leveraged or high-vol strategies.
  • by_excess — Highest Sharpe minus buy-and-hold Sharpe. Skill above the market.
  • by_hit — Highest directional hit-rate. Calibration quality. Useful when transaction costs dominate.
  • by_psr — Highest Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio. Statistical confidence Sharpe is > 0.
  • by_min_dd — Smallest maximum drawdown. Risk-controlled selection. Conservative.
  • by_compound — Highest geometric (compounded) return. Aligns with the actual maximization objective for log-utility investors.
  • by_sortino — Highest Sortino (downside-only Sharpe). Penalizes downside risk only; better than Sharpe when returns are non-normal.
  • by_recency_30d — Highest rolling 30-day Sharpe at end of OOS. Adapts to regime shifts; biased toward whichever member is hot recently.
  • by_train_composite — Highest train-time composite. Pre-OOS ranking; the natural way to pick before seeing OOS data.
📊 Risk & Quality Metrics
  • Sortino Ratio — Like Sharpe but only counts downside volatility in the denominator. Better when returns have negative skew (most equity strategies). Sortino > Sharpe is normal — they diverge as skew increases. Reference: Sortino & Price 1994 J. of Investing.
  • Max Drawdown — Largest peak-to-trough decline in equity, in %. Dollar-realised pain. Real strategies often have 1.5-2x backtest max DD live (overfit + execution). Halve your risk tolerance vs backtest.
  • Calmar Ratio — Annualised return / |max drawdown|. Geometric performance per unit of pain. Calmar > 1 is good; > 3 is exceptional.
  • Ulcer Index — RMS of drawdowns over time. Penalizes long, deep drawdowns more than brief ones. Reference: Martin 1989 'The Investor's Guide to Fidelity Funds'.
  • PSR (Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio) — Probability the OBSERVED Sharpe is > 0 given sample size, skew, and kurtosis. PSR > 0.95 = statistically certain. Critical because in-sample Sharpes can be inflated by skew/kurtosis or short windows. Reference: Bailey & López de Prado 2012 SSRN.
  • Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) — Adjusts PSR for the number of trials. If you tested 1000 strategies, the best one will have inflated Sharpe by chance — DSR corrects for this. Reference: Bailey & López de Prado 2014 J. of Portfolio Mgmt.
  • Information Ratio — Excess return / tracking error vs benchmark. Skill-above-benchmark normalized.
  • Treynor Ratio — Excess return / beta. Risk-adjusted return per unit of systematic risk.
  • VaR-95 — Loss not exceeded 95% of days. Standard regulatory risk measure.
  • CVaR-95 — Average loss on the worst 5% of days. Captures tail risk better than VaR. Coherent risk measure (Artzner et al. 1999).

⚠️ Important Caveats — Read Before Trading
  • Small-sample Sharpe inflation — Our OOS window is ~103 days. Sharpe std error ≈ 1/√(103/252) ≈ 1.56. A "true" Sharpe of +1.0 can show as +5.0 by chance ~1% of the time. Treat any single-window Sharpe with skepticism — multi-window or multi-year confirmation needed.
  • Selection bias — Selecting top-K members by metric X on the same window we evaluate the ensemble produces look-ahead bias. Real OOS Sharpe is typically 30-50% lower. Use chronological CV.
  • Transaction costs not modelled — All strategies assume zero costs. Real QQQ round-trip is ~1-2 bps; a strategy that flips daily ~60% of days bleeds ~3-5% per year just to costs. Subtract from Sharpe.
  • Theoretical option pricing — Hedging strategies use Black-Scholes with VIX as IV. Real chain prices include skew, bid-ask spread, and event premium. Realised P&L will differ.
  • No stress on extreme regimes — Backtest covers OOS forward OOS. Hyperinflation, war shocks, central-bank-digital-currency events have NEVER been seen by the model.
  • Single-asset — Strategies are QQQ-only. No portfolio diversification. Highly correlated with broad market.
  • Backtest-vs-live decay — Realised live Sharpe is typically 30-50% of backtest Sharpe due to: regime drift, execution slippage, model staleness, behavioural biases. Plan for it.
